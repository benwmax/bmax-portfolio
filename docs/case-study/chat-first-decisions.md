# Chat-first redesign — decisions

Decisions for the chat-first homepage experiment (branch `worktree-chat-first`,
draft PR #62). Kept apart from `decisions.md` while this is an experiment. If the
direction ships, the entries here move into `decisions.md` and the conflicts listed
under each one get resolved in CLAUDE.md.

Format matches `decisions.md`. Ben's calls are marked **(Ben)**. Everything else is
Claude's recommendation, which Ben accepted by approving the plan.

---

## 2026-10-29 — Try a chat-first homepage in a worktree (Ben)
- Decision: Rebuild the homepage as a full-screen chat. Case study pages go away, and
  the chat walks visitors through the work using prepared prompts and visual widgets.
  Resume, About, and Contact stay as pages.
- Reasoning: The audience is designers, hiring managers, and recruiters. A guided
  conversation can put the right project in front of each of them faster than a grid
  of cards. Building it in a worktree means the live site is untouched until Ben
  decides.
- Conflicts with: decisions.md 2026-07-18 (chat only on Home and case study pages),
  2026-07-19 (mobile FAB and overlay), 2026-07-29 (work grid as the single source of
  truth). All are superseded only if this ships.

## 2026-10-29 — Prepared tours for starter prompts, the model for typed questions
- Decision: Clicking a starter prompt plays a scripted tour (`src/chat/topics.ts`):
  short lines plus widgets, with no model call. Typed questions still go to Claude
  Haiku.
- Reasoning: Most of this audience will click, not type. A scripted tour is instant,
  costs nothing, doesn't count against the session cap, and can't misstate the work.
  The model is told which tour was just shown (`TOPIC_CONTEXT` in `api/chat.ts`), so
  follow-up questions still make sense.
- Alternatives considered: Generating every answer, which is slower, costs tokens per
  click, and risks errors on the most-viewed content.

## 2026-10-29 — Widgets come from a fixed list, requested by a text tag
- Decision: The model can end a reply with `[[show:<id>]]`. The client keeps only ids
  in `src/chat/widgetIds.ts`, at most two per reply. Widgets are React components
  built from the existing case study content, not HTML generated by the model.
- Reasoning: This keeps the "no markdown/HTML rendering without sanitization" rule
  intact, because nothing the model writes becomes markup. An invented or injected id
  renders nothing.
- Alternatives considered: Anthropic tool use. It's more structured, but complicates
  streaming for no real gain at this scale.
- Open question: The tag list in `api/lib/system-prompt.ts` and `widgetIds.ts` must
  be kept in step by hand.

## 2026-10-29 — Old case study URLs open the chat on that project (Ben)
- Decision: `/work/sabre`, `/work/usaa`, `/work/upfluent`, and `/work/sagent` stay
  live. Each opens the chat and plays that project's tour. Per-route link previews
  are unchanged.
- Reasoning: These links already appear on LinkedIn and on résumés that have been
  sent out. Breaking them costs more than keeping them.

## 2026-10-29 — Show the Portfolio Rebuild story in the chat (Ben)
- Decision: The Portfolio Rebuild content (hidden from the site on 2026-10-08) is shown
  inside the chat as part of how Ben works. It still has no page of its own. Ben will
  supply side projects done outside of work. They live in
  `src/content/side-projects.ts` and stay hidden until there's at least one entry.
- Reasoning: Visitors ask how the site was built. Ben wants to show more than client
  work.

## 2026-10-29 — Screenshots supplied after the build; nothing shows until they exist (Ben)
- Decision: Work images go in `src/content/work-images.ts` with `src` and `alt`. Until
  an entry has a file, no gallery, walkthrough step, or tour line appears.
- Reasoning: A placeholder frame in a conversation reads as broken. On a page it read
  as "coming soon"; in a reply it reads as a mistake.

## 2026-10-29 — Keep About and Resume; nav is About, Resume, Contact (Ben)
- Decision: Both pages stay. "Work" leaves the nav, because the homepage now is the
  work and the wordmark links there.

## 2026-10-29 — Merge the full Sagent case study from main
- Decision: Main shipped the full Sagent case study (PR #63) while this branch was open.
  This branch takes main's facts everywhere: ten designers, fourteen business teams,
  nine months without a director, and the Workflow builder finished in two days. Those
  replace the old "four-person team, twelve business teams" teaser lines in the Sagent,
  leadership, and design + AI tours, the "Why Ben" card, and `TOPIC_CONTEXT`.
- Also: Sagent gets no gallery beat (the work is under NDA, so no images); the
  walkthrough drops the empty "Next time" step, as `CaseStudyPage` does; and the
  design + AI tour now leads with the Workflow builder, its strongest example. The
  3+ sprint estimate is always stated as the estimate for rebuilding his earlier
  prototype, per main's honesty guard.

## 2026-10-29 — Simplify the homepage; centered input that moves down on use (Ben)
- Decision: The first screen is reduced to far less: the chat input sits centered
  vertically, the way most AI chat tools open a new session, and animates down to the
  bottom once the visitor starts engaging. The emphasis moves to Ben's UX design
  skills, with AI as how he amplifies them rather than the headline.
- Reasoning: Ben's read of the first version was that it showed too much and was hard
  to follow. Fifteen starter prompts in four groups asked the visitor to choose before
  they understood anything.
- Details (Ben's answers, same day):
  - First screen is the headline, the centered input, and four starter chips.
    Nothing else: no lede, no status badge, no topic menu.
  - Starters: "Show me his best design work", "How does he design for experts?",
    "How does AI fit his design process?", and "What's he looking for next?"
    (`STARTER_TOPICS`). The full list moves behind an "All topics" chip, which only
    appears once a conversation has started.
  - Tours are cut to two or three beats with one main widget. The full case study
    walkthrough is now a follow-up chip ("Full Sabre case study") and is never shown
    unasked.
  - The AI tour is reframed from "How does Ben use AI?" to "How does AI fit his
    design process?" It leads with designing the Upfluent AI advisor; the Claude
    split is supporting detail, and "where he overruled the AI" is a follow-up.
- Implementation: the input persists across both states. Its move from center to
  bottom is a FLIP animation (`useDockMove` in `ChatHome.tsx`), and it is skipped
  under reduced motion.
- Conflicts with: Nothing in CLAUDE.md. The positioning statement (2026-06-02) is
  unchanged.
