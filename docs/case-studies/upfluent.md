# Upfluent — Mobile-First Trading Platform

**1. Problem**
Retail options traders were stuck with two bad choices: learn a dense professional toolset built for power users, or trade at a disadvantage without it. Upfluent set out to build a trading platform — and a marketplace where educators and advisors could monetize their expertise — that closed that gap from the ground up.

**2. Role Clarity**
I was one of three designers on the team. I led the AI chatbot design effort end to end, and owned the major platform experiences: signup, account management, and core modules including Risk Analysis, where I designed a visual approach to showing how options spreads' potential outcomes shift with small adjustments — time horizons, downside risk — in a way that's far easier to read than raw numbers. The brand and visual system were a team effort I contributed to and helped converge.

**3. User Context**
Our users were retail traders who wanted to trade options like professionals but didn't have the time or background to learn a Bloomberg-style interface. They needed a platform that could meet them at their level of experience and grow with them — without dumbing down the tools that serious traders actually need.

**4. Process**
I ran competitive intelligence research to map where the market was crowded and where we could differentiate. For signup, I used card sorting and tree testing to settle the navigation hierarchy, which cut the registration flow by over 30% compared to competitors. For the chatbot, I worked with a developer to build a working prototype using the earliest ChatGPT models — before function calling, before any of the tooling that makes this easy now. Getting reliable structured output from a model that wasn't built for it was its own design problem, and I contributed both code and design to work around it.

**5. The Key Decision**
The chatbot could have gone two ways: a pure conversational interface, or a structured menu system with some AI polish. I pushed for a hybrid — the bot handles natural, free-flowing conversation, but when a user wants to act on something (set up a trade, adjust a position), it surfaces structured controls inline. The user gets the flexibility of talking to an advisor and the precision of a real interface, without choosing between them.

**6. What Was Hard or Failed**
Stakeholders wanted a full-featured desktop trading platform on par with ThinkorSwim, and they wanted that same depth on mobile. Options trading is genuinely complex — multi-leg spreads, Greeks, risk visualizations — and most of that complexity doesn't translate to a phone screen without breaking. I spent a lot of time in hard conversations about what to cut, what to simplify, and what just couldn't live on mobile the same way. The company ultimately ran into unrelated funding issues and the product never launched.

**7. Outcomes**
We delivered full platform designs and explorative AI chatbot concepts within 12 months — alongside market research, user research, and building the brand from scratch. The signup flow came in 30%+ shorter than competitor benchmarks, validated through card sort and tree testing. The chatbot prototype proved the hybrid concept worked well enough that engineering picked it up to build for real, even working from early models that weren't designed for it.

**8. What I'd Do Differently**
I'd push harder, earlier, for a way to test the chatbot with real traders — even a closed beta — instead of relying on internal prototype reviews. The hybrid concept was the right call, but I never got to see how it held up against someone actually trying to manage a live position under pressure.
